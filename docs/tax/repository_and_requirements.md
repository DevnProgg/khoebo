# Lesotho Tax Preparation Service: Repository and Requirements

**Status:** Research and implementation planning only. No application code has been changed for this phase.
**Prepared:** 4 November 2026
**Scope:** Lesotho income tax and VAT preparation for businesses that already use Khoebo's inventory, POS, bookkeeping, reporting and analytics features.
**Constraint:** Do not duplicate the existing ledger or accounting functionality. Tax calculations must consume the existing books, add a tax-adjustment layer, and persist a reproducible return snapshot.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Khoebo already has the financial raw material a tax engine needs: a business-scoped chart of accounts, double-entry journal entries, expenses, sales, sale items, inventory movements, trial balance, income statement and analytics. It has **no** tax-specific models, tax periods, taxpayer profile, tax calculations, return documents, PDF generation, document storage, fixed-asset register, payroll, or filing integration.

The recommended delivery is a **tax layer on top of the existing ledger**, not a second accounting system:

1. Add a taxpayer profile and tax-period model.
2. Add versioned Lesotho tax rule tables (rates, bands, credits, thresholds, depreciation rates).
3. Map existing accounts and transactions to tax-return lines and VAT treatments.
4. Compute returns from existing reports plus explicit tax adjustments.
5. Persist an immutable, versioned return snapshot.
6. Render a downloadable PDF from the snapshot.
7. Treat RSL e-Tax and Lekuka e-invoicing as **pluggable channels** behind interfaces. Do not assume an official e-Tax filing API exists today.

The most important product constraint discovered during research: **Lesotho's VAT e-invoicing regime is now live**. Legal Notice No. 25 of 2026 established the Lekuka / IDMS / EBS framework. RSL extended the technical integration grace period to 30 October 2026, and implementation partners report enforcement from 1 November 2026. Khoebo currently records sales but does not issue tax invoices. Any VAT-registered business using Khoebo for customer invoicing will need an accredited EBS/Lekuka path; a PDF invoice generated by Khoebo is not a compliant substitute for in-scope transactions.

---

## 2. Goals and non-goals

### Goals

- Prepare Lesotho income tax returns for **sole traders**, **partnerships** and **companies**.
- Prepare Lesotho VAT returns for VAT-registered businesses.
- Use the existing double-entry ledger as the source of truth.
- Support multiple tax years and period-specific rates without code changes.
- Produce a clear, reproducible, downloadable PDF return.
- Record manual filing, payment and acknowledgement metadata.
- Leave a clean seam for a future RSL e-Tax API and for Lekuka e-invoicing.

### Non-goals for the first release

- Replacing the existing ledger, sales, inventory or reporting modules.
- Building a full payroll product. PAYE/FBT can be added later using payroll data.
- Automating RSL filing through undocumented endpoints or browser automation.
- Generating non-compliant VAT invoices outside the Lekuka/EBS regime.
- Providing legal or tax advice. The product prepares a return from user data; the user remains responsible for review and filing.
- Supporting trusts, estates, NGOs, non-resident branches or specialised regimes in the first release unless separately scoped.

## 4. Verified Lesotho tax rules relevant to the product

The following facts were verified against RSL publications, the Government Gazette, the Income Tax Act 1993 and the VAT Act 2001. Secondary sources are identified where the primary instrument was not directly readable. **All figures must be stored as dated rule data, not hardcoded**, because amounts are amended most years.

### 4.1 Tax year and filing calendar

| Obligation | Due date | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax year | 1 April – 31 March | Standard year of assessment |
| Individual income tax return and final payment | 30 June | Applies to sole traders, employees, partners, directors |
| Company income tax return and final payment | 30 June, or last day of the third month after a substituted financial year end | Substituted period requires RSL approval |
| Provisional tax instalments | 30 September, 31 December, 31 March | Companies and individuals carrying on business |
| VAT return and payment | 20th of the following month | Tax period is one calendar month |
| PAYE return and payment | 15th of the following month | Official-secondary guidance; primary PAYE regulation not located |
| P16 employee certificate | 28 days after year end; 7 days after employment ceases | If payroll is in scope |
| Withholding tax return and payment | 15th of the following month | Official-secondary only; ITA s166 delegates timing to regulations, which remain to be pinned |
| Fringe benefits tax | Quarterly, within 14 days of quarter end | If payroll is in scope |

The **2026 filing season** for the 2025/26 year of assessment ended on 30 June 2026. The next standard season is the 2026/27 year ending 31 March 2027, with a return due 30 June 2027. The product must support prior-year returns, not only the current year.

### 4.2 Resident individual income tax (sole traders and partners)

Current 2026/27 year, effective 1 April 2026:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 20% band | First M77,760 per year (M6,480 per month) |
| 30% band | Income above M77,760 per year |
| Non-refundable tax credit | M12,240 per year (M1,020 per month) |
| Effective tax-free income | M61,200 per year (M5,100 per month) |

Prior 2025/26 year, for returns due 30 June 2026:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| 20% band | First M74,040 per year (M6,170 per month) |
| 30% band | Income above M74,040 per year |
| Non-refundable tax credit | M11,640 per year (M970 per month) |

The 2026/27 amounts come from the Income Tax (Amendment of Monetary Amounts) Regulations, 2026 (Legal Notice No. 24 of 2026), published 27 March 2026 and effective 1 April 2026. The 20% and 30% rates themselves are unchanged; only the band and credit were indexed.

A sole trader's business profit is **not** taxed separately at the business level. It is combined with the individual's other chargeable income, the individual rates are applied, and the personal credit is deducted. The product therefore needs individual-level data beyond the business ledger: other income, approved pension contributions, and any other credits or deductions.

### 4.3 Company income tax

| Income category | Rate | Source status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard / other income (resident non-manufacturing company, Lesotho branch of non-resident company) | 25% | Confirmed by RSL Tax Rates, Income Tax Act Third Schedule, and secondary practitioner guides |
| Manufacturing income, including manufacturing that promotes industrial, scientific, educational or other development in Lesotho and manufacturing for export | 10% | Income Tax Act Third Schedule; RSL Tax Rates page; Income Tax (Amendment) Act No. 2 of 2014 |
| Commercial farming income | 10% | Fourth Schedule; RSL Tax Rates page |
| Non-resident company electing under section 109 | 40% | Company return instructions; out of first-release scope |

**Resolved rate point:** manufacturing income is **10%, including manufacturing income derived exclusively from exports outside SACU**. The old export-only 0% rate was repealed by the Income Tax (Amendment) Act No. 2 of 2014. Export sales remain zero-rated for VAT, but that is a separate tax. The RSL 2025 Corporate Tax Guide was inaccessible and is not relied on for the rate. The design still needs multiple income categories and rates on one return.

Chargeable income is gross income less allowable deductions. Deductions are expenses incurred wholly and exclusively in producing taxable income. Capital expenditure is generally not deductible; depreciation is allowed under section 41 and the Sixth Schedule.

### 4.4 Partnerships

- The **partners**, not the partnership, are taxed (Income Tax Act 1993, section 75(1)).
- The partnership must file a **partnership return of income**.
- Partnership income or loss is calculated as if the partnership were a resident individual, with specified modifications.
- Each partner's distributive share equals the partner's **percentage interest** in the partnership.
- A partner's share of loss is allowed only to the extent of the adjusted cost base of the partner's interest.
- Income, expenses and losses retain their character and geographic source in the partners' hands.
- A partner's adjusted cost base is increased by allocated income and decreased by distributions and allocated losses.

The product needs partner identity, residency, ownership percentage, capital account and distributions. The partnership return is an information return; the tax liability sits with the partners.

### 4.5 VAT

| Item | Current rule |
| --- | --- |
| Standard rate | 15% |
| Electricity | 10% |
| Telecommunications | 15% |
| Zero-rated | Exports, basic foods and agricultural inputs (per RSL rate tables and VAT Act schedules) |
| Exempt | Certain specified supplies; exempt supplies do not count toward the registration threshold and do not give input credits |
| Registration threshold | M2,000,000 of taxable turnover per annum |
| Threshold instrument | Value Added Tax (Threshold for Registration of Vendors) (Amendment) Regulations, 2025, Legal Notice No. 69 of 2025, gazetted 25 April 2025 |
| Return period | One calendar month |
| Return and payment date | 20th of the following month |
| Excess credit refunds | Quarterly excess credits: application within 20 days after quarter end; regular excess credits may be claimed each tax period; other refunds within four years (VAT Act s46) |
| Voluntary registration | Allowed below the threshold, subject to Commissioner approval |

The old M850,000 threshold is **stale**. RSL's registration page and some guides still show M850,000; the gazetted 2025 amendment sets M2,000,000. The threshold applies to taxable supplies only, aggregates all businesses under one legal entity, and includes certain free supplies made in the ordinary course.

VAT payable is output VAT less input VAT. Input VAT requires a valid tax invoice and the supply must be used in making taxable supplies. The product must distinguish standard-rated, reduced-rated, zero-rated and exempt supplies, and must not treat every `Sale.tax_amount` as recoverable/output VAT without a tax code.

### 4.6 Provisional tax, PAYE, withholding tax and fringe benefits

- Provisional tax is paid by companies and individuals carrying on business.
- Three instalments are due 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. Under ITA s150, each instalment is **30% × (A − B)**, where A is the prior year's tax liability after foreign tax credit and before advance corporation tax, and B is prior-year tax paid by withholding at source. The total is 90% of (A − B). The 2018 guide's "three equal instalments" wording is inconsistent with the Act and is not controlling. [S1] [S16]
- Late provisional tax attracts additional tax at the published "specified rate" under ss192 and 194, not a permanent 3% constant. RSL secondary guidance states 22% per 12 months; store the rate as dated configuration.
- PAYE returns and payments are monthly, due the 15th under official-secondary guidance. P16 certificates are due within 28 days after year end, or 7 days after employment ceases.
- Withholding tax returns and payments are commonly monthly, due the 15th. ITA s166 defers the exact timing to regulations; the primary remittance regulation still needs to be pinned.
- Fringe benefits tax returns are quarterly, due within 14 days of the quarter end.
- Income tax late filing or payment attracts additional tax at the published specified rate. RSL secondary guidance states 22% per 12 months.

The first release can prepare income tax and VAT returns without payroll. PAYE, WHT and FBT should be separate later modules because they require employee, supplier and benefit data that Khoebo does not currently hold.

### 4.7 Depreciation and capital allowances

The Income Tax Act 1993 allows a deduction for depreciation of depreciable assets. The Sixth Schedule uses declining-balance rates:

| Group | Assets | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Automobiles, taxis, light general-purpose trucks, over-the-road tractors, special tools and devices | 25% |
| 2 | Office furniture, fixtures and equipment; computers and peripheral/data-handling equipment; buses; heavy general-purpose trucks; trailers and containers; construction equipment | 20% |
| 3 | Any depreciable asset not in another group, including mineral extraction assets | 10% |
| 4 | Railroad cars and equipment; vessels and barges; industrial buildings; engines and turbines; public utility plant | 5% |

The Sixth Schedule has **only four groups**. There is no Group 5 "Mining 100%" row in the published Act. Expenditure on mineral extraction is recovered as a Group 3 asset under section 43.

Other relevant rules:

- No deduction is allowed for the acquisition of a depreciable asset or land; the cost is recovered through depreciation.
- Repairs (other than capital improvements) are deductible.
- Minor capital equipment below the statutory amount (M50 in the published Act text) may be deducted outright under section 42.
- Accounting depreciation and tax depreciation may differ. The tax return needs a fixed-asset register and a tax depreciation schedule, not the accounting depreciation expense alone.

Khoebo has no fixed-asset register today, so this is a required new data source for companies and for sole traders/partnerships with depreciable business assets.

### 4.8 VAT e-invoicing (Lekuka / IDMS / EBS)

| Item | Current position |
| --- | --- |
| Instrument | Value Added Tax (E-Invoicing) Regulations, 2026, Legal Notice No. 25 of 2026 |
| Gazetted | 27 March 2026 (Government Gazette Vol. 71, No. 26) |
| In force | 1 April 2026 |
| Architecture | RSL Invoice Data Management System (IDMS), branded **Lekuka**, plus taxpayer-operated accredited Electronic Billing Systems (EBS) |
| Scope | Invoices, credit notes and debit notes of VAT-registered vendors that the Commissioner General requires by Gazette notice to use an EBS under reg 3. There is no automatic universal vendor mandate in the regulation text. |
| Technical elements | Accredited EBS, secure APIs, digital certificates/signatures, QR code, IDMS validation, Schedule IV invoice particulars |
| Rollout | Regulations commenced 1 April 2026. Secondary reports describe a technical grace period to 30 October 2026 and enforcement from 1 November 2026; no Gazette notice setting vendor scope/phasing was located, so treat the rollout as provisional. |
| Penalties | Schedule V fixed penalties of M50,000, M100,000, M200,000 and M300,000 according to the contravention |

This is a separate integration from income tax filing. It affects invoice validity and input VAT. Once a vendor is required by Gazette notice to use an EBS, paper/PDF invoices are not the legally contemplated instrument; invoices must come from an accredited EBS connected to IDMS. Khoebo must not claim that a locally generated PDF invoice satisfies the mandate. If Khoebo serves vendors covered by a notice, it must either become an accredited EBS or integrate with an accredited provider. Absent a notice, continue to monitor the scope rather than assuming universal coverage.

### 4.9 Record keeping and penalties

RSL expects businesses to keep records of revenue, expenses, salaries, bad debts, depreciation and other supporting documents. Income tax returns are generally accompanied by financial statements and supporting schedules. The product should therefore support document attachments and a clear audit trail.

Relevant penalties identified:

- Income tax: additional tax at the published specified rate under ss192–196. RSL secondary guidance states 22% per 12 months; the rate is not a permanent constant.
- Provisional tax: failure to pay is charged at the specified rate under s194, not the guide's 3% per month.
- VAT: late return **3% per month or part-month** (s54); late payment **3% per month or part-month** on unpaid VAT (s55); failure to register double the VAT payable during the unregistered period (s53); failure to keep proper records double the tax (s56); excess refund claims double the excess (s57).
- E-invoicing: Schedule V fixed penalties of M50,000, M100,000, M200,000 and M300,000.

Penalties are for the taxpayer's account. The product should surface deadlines and warnings but must not present estimates as legal advice.

### 4.10 Rules requiring confirmation before implementation

These are explicitly open and should be resolved with a Lesotho tax adviser or RSL before the relevant phase ships:

1. The precise definition and qualifying conditions for manufacturing and commercial farming classifications.
2. The current zero-rated and exempt VAT schedules, including the full treatment of telecommunications and electricity. Current rates are 15% standard, 10% electricity and 15% telecommunications.
3. The precise e-invoicing vendor scope, phasing and enforcement status. Reg 3 requires a Gazette notice; only secondary reports of the grace period were found.
4. Whether RSL offers any official e-Tax filing API or only the e-Tax web portal.
5. Allowable-deduction limits, loss carry-forward, thin-capitalisation, transfer-pricing and withholding-tax rules relevant to target customers.
6. The primary WHT remittance regulation and exact deadline; s166 delegates the timing.
7. Whether a small-business or turnover-tax regime exists for the target segment. No separate regime was identified in current RSL guidance; sole traders appear to be taxed under the individual regime.
8. The required PDF return format, declaration wording and signature requirements.
9. Whether the M150,000 accrual-basis threshold in the consolidated ITA s50 remains current after later amendments.
10. Whether an official revised `VAT-12` replaces the form's outdated 9% electricity line.

## 5. Taxpayer-type differences and required product behaviour

| Area | Sole trader | Partnership | Company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Taxpayer | The individual | Each partner | The company |
| Return | Individual income tax return | Partnership return plus partner individual returns | Company income tax return |
| Tax rate | Resident individual 20%/30% with personal credit | Partner's individual rates on distributive share | 25% standard; 10% manufacturing (including exports) and commercial farming |
| Business profit treatment | Added to individual's other income | Calculated at partnership level, allocated by percentage interest, taxed in partners' hands | Taxed at company level |
| Personal credit | Available once at individual level | Available in each partner's personal return, not at partnership level | Not applicable |
| Provisional tax | If carrying on business | Partners via personal provisional tax; confirm partnership-level obligation | Three instalments |
| VAT | If registered or required | If registered or required | If registered or required |
| Ownership data | Not required beyond the user | Required: partners, percentages, capital, residency | Shareholders/directors useful; dividends out of first-release scope |
| Primary Khoebo identity | `Business` + owner `User` | `Business` + `Partner` records | `Business` only |
| Key new data | Other income, personal deductions | Partner roster and allocations | Legal form, TIN, income categories, fixed assets |

### Product implications

- The tax profile must capture **legal form** and branch the return workflow.
- A partnership cannot reuse the sole-trader return unchanged; it needs an allocation schedule.
- A company must be able to split income into manufacturing, commercial farming, Lesotho other, foreign-source and property/other categories. Export is a VAT zero-rating attribute, not a separate company income tax rate. If the ledger cannot classify income automatically, the first release should allow a manual split with a reconciliation total.
- A sole trader return may need to combine business profit with employment or other income. That data is outside the current ledger and must be captured explicitly.
- VAT is a separate return for all three taxpayer types and depends on VAT registration, tax codes and valid invoices.

## 6. Data-to-tax mapping

The tax engine should read existing services rather than re-query and re-implement accounting logic. The following mapping defines the intended source for each return line.

### 6.1 Revenue and turnover

| Tax line | Source | Calculation and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross sales / turnover | `income_statement()` revenue accounts; `Sale`/`SaleItem` for detail | Revenue is credited to `4000 Sales Revenue`; discounts debit `4010 Sales Discounts`; use the ledger net figure |
| Discounts | `4010` via income statement or `Sale.discount_amount` | Contra-revenue; already reflected in ledger revenue |
| Refunds and voids | Reversal journal entries; `Sale.status = refunded/void` | Reversals use `date.today()` (`app/services/accounting.py:248`), so refunds may land in a later period than the original sale. VAT credit-note timing must be handled explicitly |
| Other income | Custom revenue accounts | Needs a tax-line mapping and possibly a manual classification |
| Export sales | Sale/customer location or manual tag | Needed for VAT zero-rating and foreign-source classification; manufacturing exports remain taxable at 10%, not 0% |
| Zero-rated / exempt sales | New tax code on sale/product | **Does not exist today**; required for VAT |

**Recommendation:** use `income_statement()` as the authoritative P&L and reconcile to `sales_summary()`/`overview()` for gross sales. Do not compute turnover solely from `Sale.total`, because that includes tax and may not match the ledger.

### 6.2 Cost of goods sold and closing stock

| Tax line | Source | Calculation and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COGS | `income_statement()` account `5000`; `SaleItem.quantity * unit_cost` | Sale posting uses `unit_cost` captured at sale time (`app/services/sales.py:68`) |
| Opening stock | Prior period closing stock | Requires a stored opening balance or reconstructed stock ledger |
| Purchases | Expense/account mapping or stock-in movements | No purchase transaction model today |
| Closing stock | `StockMovement` as of period end, or physical count | `Product.stock_quantity` is current only and must not be used for historical returns |
| Stock adjustments | `StockMovement` type `adjustment` | Needs tax treatment: write-off, shrinkage, private use, etc. |

**Recommendation:** add a period-end stock snapshot or reconstruct closing stock from `StockMovement` filtered by `created_at <= period_end`. Require a user-confirmed stock count for finalisation.

### 6.3 Expenses and deductions

| Tax line | Source | Calculation and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating expenses | `income_statement()` expense accounts | Default deductible only if wholly and exclusively for business |
| Rent, salaries, utilities, marketing, other | Seeded accounts `5200`–`5600` | Salaries may need payroll reconciliation; rent may need lease/withholding review |
| Depreciation | Fixed-asset register | **Does not exist today**; accounting depreciation is not the same as tax depreciation |
| Non-deductible expenses | `TaxAdjustment` | Fines, private expenses, capital items, income tax, unsupported expenses |
| Donations | `TaxAdjustment` | Confirm deductibility limits |
| Interest and finance costs | Expense accounts or new data | Confirm thin-capitalisation and interest-limitation rules |
| Bad debts | `TaxAdjustment` | Confirm write-off evidence requirements |

`Expense` currently stores a description, amount, date, payment method and account. It does **not** store supplier, supplier TIN, invoice number, VAT amount, tax code or a document link. That is enough for bookkeeping but not enough for a defensible VAT input claim or a detailed tax schedule. The first release can use account-level mapping and manual adjustments; a later phase should enrich expenses.

### 6.4 VAT

| Tax line | Source | Calculation and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output VAT | `Sale.tax_amount` and tax payable credits | Only valid if each sale/line has a VAT tax code; current `tax_rate` is a free numeric field |
| Input VAT | `Expense` VAT data or a new purchase/invoice model | **Not currently captured** |
| Zero-rated sales | New sale/product tax code | Required |
| Exempt sales | New sale/product tax code | Required; no input credit |
| Credit notes / refunds | Refund reversals and `Sale.status` | Need period-accurate credit notes |
| Net VAT payable/refund | Output less input | Monthly, due 20th of following month |

**Recommendation:** introduce tax codes at product and sale-line level, plus VAT fields on expenses/purchases. Until then, VAT return preparation should be marked **experimental/manual** and should not silently treat all `Sale.tax_amount` as output VAT.

### 6.5 Trial balance and balance sheet

| Tax line | Source | Calculation and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash and bank | `1000` | Existing ledger treats all sale payments as cash |
| Accounts receivable | `1010` | Account exists but no AR lifecycle |
| Inventory | `1200` | Ledger balance should reconcile to stock valuation |
| Accounts payable | `2000` | Account exists but no AP lifecycle |
| Tax payable | `2200` | Cumulative sales tax; not a VAT-return substitute |
| Owner's equity | `3000` | Sole trader capital/drawings need separate analysis |
| Retained earnings / equity | Custom accounts | Company return reconciliation |

Use `trial_balance(as_of=period_end)` for balance-sheet checks. The income statement covers a date range; the trial balance can be as-of a date.

### 6.6 Taxpayer identity and responsibility

| Data | Source | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal name | `Business.name` | Return header |
| Trading name / slug | `Business.slug` | Reference only |
| Owner / members | `BusinessMember` with `User` | Preparer, nominated officer, partner identity |
| Contact | `User.email`, `User.phone_number` | Return contact details |
| Currency | `Business.currency` | Must be LSL |
| Tax identifiers | **New `TaxProfile`** | TIN, VAT number, taxpayer type |

## 7. Gap analysis: data and capabilities to add

### 7.1 Taxpayer profile

Required fields:

- Legal form: `sole_trader`, `partnership`, `company` (extensible).
- Legal/trading name and registered address.
- TIN (taxpayer identification number).
- VAT registration status, VAT number, registration effective date, voluntary/compulsory flag.
- Tax year end (default 31 March; companies may have an approved substituted period).
- Residency status and, for companies, place of incorporation/management.
- Sector/industry and income categories: manufacturing (including export manufacturing), commercial farming, Lesotho other, foreign-source, property/other.
- Nominated officer / public officer and preparer details.
- Accounting basis (cash or accrual), if relevant.
- RSL e-Tax connection reference (not a plaintext password).
- Consent/declaration metadata.

### 7.2 Tax periods and obligations

- `TaxPeriod` for each business, tax type and period.
- Derived due dates from the rules table.
- Status: open, computed, finalised, filed, amended, paid.
- Optional period lock to prevent silent changes after finalisation.
- Filing season label (e.g. 2025/26, 2026/27).
- Obligation flags: income tax, VAT, provisional, PAYE, WHT, FBT.

### 7.3 Tax rules and rates

- `TaxRuleSet` keyed by tax year and tax type.
- Individual brackets and credit per year.
- Corporate rates by income category.
- VAT rates and registration threshold.
- Depreciation groups and rates.
- Provisional tax instalment percentages and dates.
- Penalty/interest reference data for warnings only.
- Effective-from and effective-to dates; never overwrite historical rules.

### 7.4 Fixed assets and depreciation

- Asset name, category/group, acquisition date, cost, opening written-down value.
- Disposal date, proceeds, gain/loss.
- Accounting depreciation vs tax depreciation.
- Period depreciation charge and closing written-down value.
- Source document link.

### 7.5 Partners and ownership

- Partner name or linked `User`, TIN, residency.
- Ownership percentage, effective dates, capital contributed.
- Distributive share of income/loss, drawings, adjusted cost base.
- Changes in partnership constitution during the year.

### 7.6 Individual-level data for sole traders and partners

- Other employment income, pension income, investment income.
- Approved pension/retirement contributions.
- Other deductions, credits and foreign tax credits.
- Prior-year assessed losses or credits.
- Provisional tax and withholding tax already paid.

### 7.7 VAT-specific data

- Tax code on `Product` and `SaleItem` (standard/reduced/zero/exempt/out-of-scope).
- Customer name, address, TIN/VAT number for B2B invoices.
- Supplier name, TIN/VAT number, invoice number, invoice date, VAT amount on expenses/purchases.
- Credit/debit notes linked to original invoices.
- Import/reverse-charge transactions.
- Input VAT apportionment for mixed taxable/exempt businesses.
- Evidence of valid tax invoices and document storage.
- Lekuka/EBS invoice identifier, QR code, validation status.

### 7.8 Tax adjustments and reconciliation

- Adjustment category, description, amount, direction, tax period.
- Source and evidence document.
- Prepared/approved by, status.
- Reconciliation lines: ledger amount, adjustment, return amount, variance.
- Block finalisation if material unreconciled differences remain.

### 7.9 Payments, filings and documents

- Tax payments: type, period, date, amount, method, reference, receipt.
- Filing attempts: channel (PDF/manual, e-Tax future, Lekuka future), status, request/response, RSL reference.
- Documents: financial statements, invoices, receipts, WHT certificates, P16, prior returns, correspondence.
- Immutable return snapshot and PDF hash.
- Audit log for view, compute, finalise, download and file actions.

## 8. Proposed architecture and integration design

### 8.1 Design principles

1. **One ledger.** Tax reads `JournalEntry`/`JournalLine` and the existing report services. It does not create a parallel revenue or expense ledger.
2. **Snapshot, don't recompute history.** A finalised return stores all input lines and computed values. The PDF is rendered from the snapshot, not from live queries.
3. **Rules are data.** Rates, bands, credits, thresholds and depreciation rates live in dated rule tables.
4. **Adjustments are explicit.** Every difference between ledger profit and taxable profit is a persisted, explainable adjustment.
5. **Deterministic money.** Use `Decimal` and the existing `money()` quantisation. No floats.
6. **Tenant isolation and roles on every route.** Reuse `get_membership()` and `require_roles()`.
7. **Filing is a channel.** Calculation is independent of whether the return is downloaded, manually filed or submitted through an API.
8. **No silent legal assumptions.** Unsupported or unconfirmed rules produce a warning or block finalisation.

### 8.6 PDF / downloadable return flow

The PDF is a **presentation of a finalised snapshot**, not a live report.

1. User selects tax type and period.
2. System computes a draft from the ledger plus adjustments and shows reconciliation warnings.
3. User reviews and resolves blockers.
4. User finalises: the return becomes immutable and gets a version number, snapshot hash and finalised timestamp.
5. User downloads the PDF. The PDF is generated from the snapshot and can be regenerated identically.
6. User records manual filing: date, RSL reference, payment reference, acknowledgement document.
7. Later ledger changes do not alter the finalised return; the user must create an amended version.

PDF contents:

- Taxpayer legal name, trading name, TIN, VAT number (if applicable), address.
- Return type, tax year/period, currency LSL, version, status.
- Computation summary: gross income, deductions, chargeable income, tax before credits, credits, provisional/withholding payments, balance due or refund.
- Detailed schedules: revenue, COGS/stock, expenses, fixed assets/depreciation, partner allocation, VAT output/input (as applicable).
- Source references: ledger period, report run timestamp, snapshot hash.
- Preparer and nominated-officer details, declaration, date and signature line.
- Page numbers, generated timestamp and a clear "prepared from Khoebo records; not filed with RSL unless separately submitted" disclaimer.

Technical notes:

- No PDF library is currently installed. Select one in the implementation phase after licence and deployment review (e.g. WeasyPrint for HTML/CSS templates or ReportLab for programmatic layout).
- Store the PDF as an immutable `Document` with SHA-256. If storage is not available, the snapshot must be sufficient to regenerate it byte-for-byte or deterministically.
- Serve downloads with `Content-Disposition: attachment`, tenant checks, role checks and audit logging.
- Keep templates versioned alongside the return schema.

### 8.7 Future RSL e-Tax API readiness

RSL's public e-Tax platform is a web portal for filing CIT, IIT, VAT and PAYE. No official public filing API was identified. The design should therefore:

- Define a `TaxFilingChannel` interface: `submit(snapshot)`, `get_status(reference)`, `download_acknowledgement(reference)`.
- Implement `ManualPdfChannel` for the first release.
- Add `RslEtaxChannel` only when RSL publishes an official API and access terms.
- Persist every attempt in `FilingAttempt` with an idempotency key, request/response metadata and status.
- Keep a stable internal canonical return schema, with a separate mapping layer to any RSL schema.
- Version both the return schema and the tax rules.
- Store credentials in a secret manager or encrypted store; never in plaintext or logs. Use per-business connections with least privilege.
- Add retries with exponential backoff, circuit breaking, rate limiting and clear user-visible errors.
- Do not use browser automation or undocumented endpoints without written confirmation; that creates legal, security and maintenance risk.

### 8.8 Lekuka e-invoicing readiness (separate workstream)

Lekuka is not an income tax return API. It is an invoice-data clearance system. The tax product should:

- Treat e-invoicing as a **separate integration** from return filing.
- Add tax codes and customer/supplier tax identifiers before attempting VAT return automation.
- Integrate only through an RSL-accredited EBS, or become accredited, if Khoebo will issue in-scope invoices.
- Persist the EBS invoice identifier, QR code, validation status and any rejection reasons.
- Never label a Khoebo-generated PDF as a valid VAT invoice for in-scope transactions.
- Keep e-invoice data and VAT return data reconciled but independently auditable.

- Implement income tax and corporate tax calculations from `income_statement()` plus adjustments.
- Add reconciliation checks against the ledger.

- Implement PDF rendering from the snapshot.

- Implement partnership income calculation and allocation.

## 10. Testing strategy

### Unit tests

- Individual brackets and credits for each tax year, including the 2025/26 and 2026/27 transition.
- Corporate rate selection by income category, including mixed income.
- Partnership allocation by percentage interest, loss limitation and adjusted cost base.
- VAT output/input, zero-rated, exempt and credit-note treatment.
- Depreciation by asset group, disposals and opening/closing WDV.
- Money rounding and quantisation.
- Deadline derivation for standard and substituted year ends.

### Integration tests

- Create business, sales, expenses, assets and partners; compute and finalise a return.
- Reconcile return lines to `income_statement()` and `trial_balance()`.
- Confirm finalised returns are immutable and amendments create a new version.
- Confirm PDF generation uses the snapshot and does not change when live data changes.
- Confirm tenant isolation: another business cannot read or download a return.
- Confirm role enforcement for compute, finalise, download and file.
- Confirm audit events are written for sensitive actions.

### Migration tests

- Follow the existing `tests/test_migrations.py` pattern for upgrade/downgrade of new tax tables.
- Verify SQLite and PostgreSQL compatibility for UUIDs, enums and numeric types.

### Regression and golden tests

- Maintain fixed scenarios for a sole trader, a partnership and a company with expected return lines.
- Store expected PDF snapshots or hashes.
- Re-run historical-year tests whenever rule tables change, to prove old rules were not overwritten.

### Future channel tests

- Contract tests for the `TaxFilingChannel` interface.
- Fake HTTP server tests for any future RSL adapter.
- E-invoicing adapter tests against a sandbox EBS/IDMS environment when available.

## 11. Security, tenancy and compliance

- Every tax route must use `get_membership()`; never accept a business ID without a membership check.
- Default write access to owner/admin; consider owner/admin-only read access because returns contain TINs and financial detail.
- Audit every view, compute, finalise, download, file and delete action.
- Store TINs, VAT numbers and tax documents as sensitive data; do not log them in plaintext.
- Store credentials for RSL/Lekuka in a secret manager or encrypted store, not in the database or environment files committed to source control.
- Use signed, time-limited download URLs if documents are stored externally.
- Define retention and deletion rules for tax documents and return snapshots.
- Keep the legal disclaimer visible in the UI and PDF. The product prepares returns; it does not provide tax advice or guarantee RSL acceptance.
- Do not file through unofficial channels. Respect RSL terms of use.

## 12. Key risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stale tax rates or thresholds | Wrong returns | Dated rule tables, effective dates, regression tests, visible rule version |
| RSL guidance inconsistencies | Wrong corporate/provisional treatment | Confirm with RSL/adviser; encode as configurable rules; warn users |
| No tax codes on sales/products | VAT cannot be computed reliably | Add tax codes before VAT phase; label early VAT output as draft/manual |
| Current stock quantity is not historical | Wrong closing stock | Reconstruct from `StockMovement` or require period-end count |
| Refunds post to current date | Period mismatch | Add period-aware credit notes or explicit refund-period adjustment |
| Cash-only expense posting / no AR-AP lifecycle | Accrual mismatches | Capture accounting basis; add accrual adjustments; document limitation |
| No e-invoicing compliance | Invalid VAT invoices; penalties | Integrate with accredited EBS or clearly hand off; do not issue PDF VAT invoices |
| No official e-Tax API | Cannot automate filing | Build channel abstraction; ship manual PDF flow first |
| Sensitive taxpayer data | Privacy/security breach | Tenant isolation, role checks, audit logs, encryption, retention policy |
| Overbuilding before legal clarity | Wasted work | Phase 0 sign-off; start with income tax and PDF, defer VAT/e-invoicing |

## 13. Recommended first release

The smallest useful release is:

1. Taxpayer profile for sole traders and companies.
2. Tax periods for the standard 1 April – 31 March year.
3. Versioned individual and corporate rule tables.
4. Income tax computation from the existing income statement plus explicit adjustments.
5. A finalised, immutable return snapshot.
6. A downloadable PDF with schedules and reconciliation.
7. Manual filing and payment metadata.
8. Full tenant isolation, owner/admin permissions and audit logging.

Defer partnerships, VAT, fixed assets and payroll until the income tax and PDF flow is validated. Treat Lekuka e-invoicing as a separate workstream with its own legal and accreditation dependencies.
